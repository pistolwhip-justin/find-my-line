# Find-My-Line — Positioning and Mission

## Purpose

This document establishes the current positioning and mission for Find-My-Line. It is intentionally concise and should guide product, website, marketing, documentation, and public communications.

## Core positioning

> **Find-My-Line finds the line → your navigation tool follows it.**

Find-My-Line is a bikepacking route-discovery and route-generation product. It is not intended to replace navigation applications. Its job is to discover and generate better route candidates from rider intent and real geographic evidence, then hand the selected line to the rider's preferred navigation tool.

## Mission

> **Help riders discover better bikepacking routes that conventional route planners overlook, using real geographic data, rider intent, and real-world riding experience.**

## What Find-My-Line is

- A route-discovery system for bikepacking and adventure-oriented riding.
- A route generator that considers rider intent rather than treating every bicycle trip the same.
- A system for comparing multiple practical route candidates and their tradeoffs.
- An evidence-driven system that combines geographic data with real-world rider experience.
- An interoperability-first product that lets riders take the resulting route into the navigation tools they already use.

## What Find-My-Line is not

- Not a turn-by-turn navigation application.
- Not a voice-navigation replacement.
- Not a live traffic navigation service.
- Not a continuous background navigation replacement.
- Not a proprietary global basemap that attempts to replace established mapping ecosystems.
- Not a general-purpose social network.
- Not a route-library replacement whose value depends on locking riders into one ecosystem.

## Messaging principles

### Discover rather than navigate

The product's distinctive job is finding the line. Navigation is downstream.

### Routes rather than maps

Maps are an input and a visualization. The primary product output is a usable route candidate.

### Rider intent rather than generic bike routing

A bikepacker may care about surface, traffic, remoteness, technical difficulty, climbing, legal access, connectivity, scenery, resupply, and bailout options differently from an ordinary bicycle commuter. Find-My-Line should represent those preferences explicitly.

### Real-world suitability rather than theoretical optimization

The shortest or fastest connected path is not necessarily the route a bikepacker wants. Route evaluation should consider evidence about actual suitability.

### Multiple choices rather than one magic answer

When materially different routes satisfy the request, show alternatives and explain their tradeoffs rather than pretending there is one objectively correct line.

### Evidence rather than AI imagination

AI may translate rider language into structured preferences and explain/evaluate candidate routes. AI must not invent route geometry or unsupported geographic facts. Actual connected route geometry comes from the routing/data system.

### Interoperability rather than lock-in

GPX remains a permanent interoperability layer. Additional formats and direct integrations can reduce friction without making Find-My-Line dependent on a particular navigation provider.

## Audience

The initial audience is riders who actively care about route quality beyond ordinary road-bike routing, especially:

- Bikepackers
- Gravel riders
- Adventure cyclists
- Experienced off-road cyclists
- Riders who build or modify their own routes
- Regional riders with detailed knowledge of local surfaces, access, and conditions

Audience expansion should follow evidence from actual use rather than being assumed in advance.

## Product promise

Find-My-Line should help a rider answer:

> **"Given where I want to go and how I want to ride, what lines are actually worth considering?"**

It should then provide enough evidence and explanation for the rider to choose among them and enough interoperability for the chosen line to continue into the rider's normal navigation workflow.

## Brand guardrails

Public messaging should not imply capabilities the product does not provide. In particular, avoid describing Find-My-Line as a navigation replacement, autonomous route authority, or infallible AI route planner.

Claims about route quality should be grounded in documented data, testing, and rider evidence. Community popularity is not by itself proof of route quality.

## Relationship to the business

This positioning is the product-level expression of the broader business strategy. Business, pricing, marketing, website, community, and partnership decisions should reinforce the distinction between **finding the line** and **following the line**.

## Status
